Setup takes about ten minutes. You'll connect power, get the controller onto
your Wi-Fi and choose a password for its settings. You can add Home
Assistant afterwards.

## What you need

- **A 24 V DC power supply** with an XT60 lead. Anything from 20 to 28 V
  works. Choose its size by how much your devices draw in total, not by six
  times 100 W. The default power budget is 360 W (15 A at 24 V). If your
  supply is smaller, you'll lower the budget to match in step 5 below.
- **USB-C cables.** An ordinary cable carries up to 3 A (60 W). For more
  than 60 W at 20 V a device needs a 5 A cable with an e-marker chip, which
  is usually sold as a "100 W" or "240 W" cable.
- **A phone or computer with Bluetooth** for Wi-Fi setup: Chrome or Edge
  on a desktop or Android device, or the Home Assistant app on an iPhone or
  iPad.

<Aside type="caution">
Connect the XT60 lead before you switch the supply on, and switch the
supply off before you unplug it.
</Aside>

## Steps

<Steps>

1. **Connect power.** Plug the power supply's XT60 lead into the back of
   the chassis, then switch the supply on. The port lights sweep from left
   to right, then each port glows amber, which means it is ready and
   waiting for a device. Ports are numbered 1 to 6 from left to right when
   you face the front panel.

2. **Get it on your network.** A new controller advertises itself over
   Bluetooth until it has Wi-Fi details. Follow
   [Wi-Fi setup](/power-manifold/setup/wifi-provisioning/), either from that
   page in your browser or with the Home Assistant app. While the controller
   is waiting for setup, a blue light runs across the ports every few
   seconds.

3. **Open the web page.** When Wi-Fi setup finishes, press **Next** on the
   same phone or computer. This opens the controller's web page with
   settings unlocked for ten minutes. Later you can find the page at
   `http://pwrman.local/`, or at the new name if you change it.

4. **Choose an API token.** Open **Settings** near the bottom of the page
   and set an **API token**.
   This is the password for changing anything from the web page or the API.
   You'll need it on any other device, so keep it somewhere safe. Once the
   token is saved, the ten-minute setup window closes.

5. **Set the power budget.** Still in **Settings**, set the **Chassis budget**
   to your supply's rating in watts, or a little under it. See
   [Sharing power between ports](/power-manifold/guide/sharing-power/) for
   how the budget is used.

6. **Optional: give it a name and connect Home Assistant.** Set a device
   name if you have more than one Power Manifold. Fill in your MQTT broker
   if you use Home Assistant; see
   [Home Assistant](/power-manifold/guide/home-assistant/). Press **Reboot**
   to apply a new name or broker.

</Steps>

That's it. Plug in a device and its port light turns blue or green.

## Using wired Ethernet

The controller card has an Ethernet port. Plug in a cable and the controller
gets an address from your router automatically. When both Ethernet and
Wi-Fi are connected, it uses Ethernet and keeps Wi-Fi as a backup.

You still need to do Wi-Fi setup once, because that is how the settings
get unlocked the first time. After that, set a fixed address if you want
one. See [Network settings](/power-manifold/integrations/network/).
