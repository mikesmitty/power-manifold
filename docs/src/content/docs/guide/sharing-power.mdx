Six ports at 100 W would need a 600 W supply. Most setups never draw that
much at once, so Power Manifold can run from a smaller supply and divide its
power between the ports. You tell it how much power the supply has (the **budget**) and which
ports matter most (their **priority**).

## The budget

Set the **Chassis budget** in the web interface's **Settings** or in Home
Assistant. Use your power supply's rating in watts, or a little less. The
default is 360 W.

When a device plugs in, it agrees on a voltage and current with its port,
for example 20 V at 3 A. Power Manifold sets that much power aside, 60 W in
this example, even if the device draws less at the moment. Laptops and other
devices can jump to their full power at any time, so setting aside the full
amount is what keeps the supply from being overloaded.

A port with a blade but nothing plugged in sets aside 15 W, enough for a
device to plug in and start at 5 V.

The web interface shows both numbers on each port's card: the large figure is
what the device uses right now, and the contract under it is what is set
aside for it. The budget bar at the top adds them up for the whole chassis.

## When there isn't enough

If a newly plugged-in device would take the total over the budget, Power
Manifold makes room:

1. It turns down lower-priority ports, starting with the lowest. It never
   takes a port below 15 W, and never turns down a port with
   [Protect running device](/guide/port-settings/#protect-running-device)
   on.
2. If that isn't enough, it gives the new device whatever is left.

A port that has been turned down keeps powering its device, only with less
power. Its light pulses slowly, and its state shows `throttled`. The
device is sent a new, smaller offer and switches to it. Some devices may
briefly lose power while they renegotiate: if a device doesn't answer the
new offer quickly enough, the USB Power Delivery standard requires the port
to reset the connection, which cuts its power for a moment. For devices
that could be harmed by that, turn on
[Protect running device](/guide/port-settings/#protect-running-device).

Nothing is switched off to save power.

When power frees up, for example when another device is unplugged or a
port with [switch off when charged](/guide/port-settings/#switching-off-when-charged)
turns itself off, the turned-down ports get it back, highest priority
first. A device that has finished charging but stays plugged in keeps its
share, because it can start drawing again at any time.

## Priorities

Each port has a priority from 0 to 255, where **0 is the most important**.
By default a port's priority is its port number, so port 1 is the most
important and port 6 the least.

Ports with the same priority never take power from each other. The one
that plugged in first keeps what it has.

Set priorities in Home Assistant (a number for each port), over
[MQTT](/integrations/mqtt/) or with the
[HTTP API](/integrations/http-api/).

**Example:** a 150 W supply powers four Raspberry Pi 5s on ports 1 to 4,
which take 25 W each, and a 100 W laptop on port 6. Give the Pis priority 0
so they always get what they ask for, and leave the laptop at 6. The Pis
take 100 W of the budget, so the laptop is offered the rest and charges more
slowly. If a Pi is unplugged, the laptop gets its share. Turning on
[Protect running device](/guide/port-settings/#protect-running-device) for
the Pis as well means their power is settled when they are plugged in and
never changes while they run.

## When the input voltage drops

If the supply voltage falls below 20 V, every port is limited to 3 A (60 W
at most) until the voltage has been above 20.5 V for 30 seconds. Devices are offered less, but nothing is switched
off. While this is happening the web interface and Home Assistant show a
problem saying the ports are capped.

If you see this often, your supply is too small for your devices, or its
cable is too thin. Lower the budget or use a bigger supply. See
[Troubleshooting](/guide/troubleshooting/#ports-capped-at-3-a).

## The fan

The fan switches on automatically under any of these conditions:

- when the total power reaches 80 W, staying on until it falls to 60 W;
- while any port delivers more than 3 A;
- while any blade's converter is at 65 °C or hotter, staying on until all
  of them have cooled to 55 °C.

You can change the first two rules in **Settings**, or force the fan on or
off from the web interface or Home Assistant.

<Aside type="caution">
Forcing the fan off disables the temperature rule too. Leave it on
**auto** unless you are testing.
</Aside>
