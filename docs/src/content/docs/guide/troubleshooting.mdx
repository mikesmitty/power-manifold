## Where problems show up

When something needs attention, Power Manifold says so in several places:

- a red line near the top of the [web interface](/guide/web-interface/);
- the **Problem** sensor in Home Assistant, whose `detail` attribute says
  what is wrong;
- the lights: a red, fast-blinking light for a port that faulted, the
  chassis light (left of port 1) blinking red when the input voltage is out
  of range, and the chassis light breathing red when there's no network.

The web interface's **Fault log** keeps a history of past faults.

## A port blinks red

The port shut itself off to protect your device or the blade. The usual
causes are a device drawing more current than the port offers, a short
in the cable or device, or a blade that got too hot. The fault log entry
says which one.

The port tries again by itself after 5 seconds. If the cause is still there,
it faults again, and the light keeps blinking.

What to try:

1. Unplug the device, wait for the light to turn white, and plug it back in.
2. Try a different cable. A damaged cable is a common cause.
3. If the log says *plug-hot* or *converter-hot*, check that nothing blocks
   the vents, especially the fan's on the right side, and that the fan is
   on **auto**. Let it cool down.
4. If the same device faults on every port, the device is the problem.

## A device doesn't charge, or charges slowly

- **Light white with the device plugged in:** the port doesn't see the
  device. Reseat the cable at both ends, or try another cable. Some cheap
  USB-A to USB-C adapters and cables don't work with USB-C chargers.
- **Light off:** the port is switched off. Turn it on with the switch on its card on the
  [web interface](/guide/web-interface/#the-port-cards) or in Home Assistant.
  If you didn't switch it off, check whether
  [switch off once charged or the sleep timer](/guide/port-settings/#switching-off-when-charged)
  is on, or whether the port is set to stay **off** at power-up.
- **Light pulsing slowly:** the port is turned down because the chassis is
  at its budget. See [Sharing power](/guide/sharing-power/).
- **Laptop charges slower than expected:** check the port's
  [voltage cap and current limit](/guide/port-settings/#voltage-cap).
  Laptops need 20 V, and more than 60 W needs a 5 A cable.

## Ports capped at 3 A

The problem line says *bus voltage sagging, ports capped at 3 A*. The input
voltage from your power supply fell below 20 V, so every port was limited
to 60 W to take load off it. It lifts by itself 30 seconds after the
voltage recovers.

If it happens often:

- your supply is too small for your devices, so lower the
  [budget](/guide/sharing-power/#the-budget) to match it, or
  use a bigger supply;
- or the cable from the supply is too long or too thin, so use a shorter,
  thicker one.

If the voltage keeps falling, Power Manifold disconnects all ports at
about 17.7 V and reconnects them when the voltage returns. The fault log
records the low voltage.

## Bus voltage out of range

The chassis light, left of port 1, blinks red, and the problem line says
*bus voltage low* or *bus voltage high*. The input voltage from your power
supply is outside Power Manifold's rating of 24 V +20 %/−15 %. The ports keep
their power, but check the power supply, its output setting and its cable.
The light goes back to dim green once the voltage is back in range.

## A port shows *silent* or *not answering*

The blade stopped responding to the controller. The port keeps its power
and the controller keeps asking. The readings you see are the last ones it
got. This usually clears by itself. If it doesn't, switch the port off and
on again. If that doesn't help, switch the power supply off and on again.

## No network

The chassis light, left of port 1, breathing red means the controller has
no network address.

- **Wi-Fi:** check that the network is up. After five minutes off the
  network the controller opens Wi-Fi setup, so you can
  [enter new Wi-Fi details](/setup/wifi-provisioning/) if
  your network changed.
- **Ethernet:** check the cable and that your router hands out addresses.
  If you set a fixed address, check that it is still free and correct.

The ports keep working with no network. Only the web interface, Home Assistant
and the API are unavailable.

## Can't reach the web interface

- Try the IP address from your router instead of `pwrman.local`. Some
  networks and older Android phones don't resolve `.local` names.
- If you renamed the device, the address is `http://<new-name>.local/`.
- With [HTTPS](/integrations/https/) on, use the name on the certificate.
  If that name no longer resolves, open the controller by its IP address
  and accept the browser's warning that the certificate is for another
  name. From there you can install a new certificate, or set **HTTPS** to
  *Off* in **Settings**.

## Forgot the API token

You can't recover the token. To set a new one, do a
[factory reset](/guide/front-panel/#the-button) and repeat
[first-time setup](/setup/first-time-setup/). If you have a
settings export that includes secrets, you can import it after the reset.

## Other problems the controller reports

| Message | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| *trial firmware uncommitted* | A firmware update is on trial. It clears within a few seconds when the update succeeds. See [Updates](/guide/updates/). |
| *wired link down* | The Ethernet cable is unplugged or the switch is off. Wi-Fi is carrying traffic for now. |
| *on battery* / UPS battery fault | Your [UPS power supply](/integrations/ups/) lost AC power or reports a battery problem. |
| *bus voltage* out of range | The input voltage is outside the rated range. See [Bus voltage out of range](#bus-voltage-out-of-range). |
| *HTTPS certificate expires in N days* / *expired* | The certificate renewal has stopped. Check the acme.sh or certbot job. See [HTTPS](/integrations/https/#expiry). |
| *HTTPS on but no certificate* | The stored certificate is missing or does not load, so the controller serves plain HTTP. Install it again. |
