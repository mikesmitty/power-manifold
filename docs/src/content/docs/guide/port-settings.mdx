Each port has its own settings. You can change them in the web interface's
**Settings**, in [Home Assistant](/power-manifold/guide/home-assistant/), over
[MQTT](/power-manifold/integrations/mqtt/) or with the
[HTTP API](/power-manifold/integrations/http-api/). Changes apply at once,
even to a device that is already plugged in.

## Name

Give each port a name, such as `pi-cluster-1` or `Desk phone`, up to 23
characters. The name shows in the web interface and in Home Assistant, where
the entities are renamed to match: *Port 3 power* becomes *Desk phone
power*. Renaming doesn't change any entity IDs, so your automations keep
working.

Leave the name blank to go back to `Port 1`, `Port 2` and so on.

## On or off at power-up

Choose what each port does when Power Manifold is switched on, for example
after a power cut:

- **on** (the default): the port powers up.
- **off**: the port stays off until you switch it on.
- **last**: the port comes back the way you last left it.

When the chassis powers up, the ports come on one at a time, a quarter of a
second apart, in [priority](/power-manifold/guide/sharing-power/#priorities)
order. This stops six devices from starting all at once.

## Voltage cap

The voltage cap is the highest voltage a port offers. Choose 5, 9, 12, 15
or 20 V. The default is 20 V, which offers everything.

Use a lower cap to protect a device that shouldn't get a high voltage. For
example:

- **5 V** for a device with a USB-C trigger board or adapter that you don't
  fully trust.
- **9 V** for a phone you'd rather charge gently.

## Current limit

The current limit is the most current a port offers at any voltage, from
500 to 5000 mA. Power scales with the voltage the device picks: a 3000 mA
limit means 15 W at 5 V and 60 W at 20 V.

The limit only sets what the port offers. A device that wants more simply
gets less. It isn't cut off.

<Aside type="note" title="Cables limit current too">
Above 3 A the cable must have a 5 A e-marker chip. The port checks the cable
and offers 5 A only on a cable that can carry it. With an ordinary cable a
port stops at 3 A (60 W at 20 V), whatever the limit says.
</Aside>

## Switching off when charged

The controller notices when a device has finished charging. That happens
when the device draws less than **500 mW** for **10 minutes**. The port
light turns white, as if nothing were plugged in, and the port shows *charged*. If the device starts
drawing again for that long, it counts as charging again. You can change
both numbers in **Settings**, or set the power to 0 to turn detection off.

Two per-port options build on this:

- **Switch off once charged** turns the port off as soon as the device is
  charged. Use it to stop phones and tablets from sitting at 100 % all
  night.
- **Sleep timer** turns the port off a set number of minutes after you plug
  a device in, whether it has finished charging or not, up to 24 hours.

When either one switches a port off, it stays off until you switch it on
again. A port set to **last** at power-up remembers that it was off.

<Aside type="tip">
**Switch off once charged** and the sleep timer are meant for devices with a
battery. Leave them off on ports that power a Raspberry Pi or another
computer that should stay on.
</Aside>

## Priority

Each port also has a priority, which decides which ports are turned down
first when devices ask for more power than the supply can give. See
[Sharing power between ports](/power-manifold/guide/sharing-power/).
